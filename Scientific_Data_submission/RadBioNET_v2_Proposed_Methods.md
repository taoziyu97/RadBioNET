# RadBioNET v2: prospective Methods for an evidence-linked radiation-response dataset

**Status, 13 November 2026.** This is an implementation protocol and manuscript-ready Methods draft, not a report of work already completed. Future-tense procedures and all bracketed run-specific fields must be replaced with the actual executed process, dates, versions and observed counts before submission. The existing 15,720-row RadBioNET workbook is an input to reconciliation and is not a reference standard for correctness. The proposed release will be a new version with explicit links to legacy records.

## Methods

### Study design, scope and unit of annotation

RadBioNET v2 will be a versioned, literature-derived dataset of reported relationships between a biological or pharmacological factor and the response of a cancer or normal-tissue system to ionizing radiation. The unit of the curated relation table will be one **publication × experimental comparison × factor role and perturbation × irradiation arm × measured outcome × effect direction**. A publication may contribute several comparisons and an irradiation arm may contribute several outcomes. A relation will not be created solely because a factor and an outcome term co-occur in a title, abstract or article. Each accepted record will point to the text, table or figure that supports the factor, intervention or observation, irradiation context, outcome and inferred direction. Bibliographic and publication-level descriptors will be stored separately from arm-level and relation-level descriptors. Explicitly unresolved fields will remain unresolved.

The target material will be original reports published from 1 January 2000 through the frozen search date [SEARCH_DATE], including in-vitro, animal and human studies relevant to cancer radiotherapy or radiobiology. A report will be eligible if it contains original observations or experimental results that permit at least one factor-specific radiation-response relationship to be evaluated. Planned eligible outcome families are tumour cell survival or control, normal-tissue injury, treatment toxicity, DNA damage or repair, radiation-linked immune response and other defined biological responses. Reviews, editorials, commentaries, protocols without results, retracted reports, studies only about non-ionizing exposure, treatment planning or beam physics without a factor-specific biological outcome, and reports lacking a cancer or cancer-treatment context will be excluded from the relation table. A clinical study need not demonstrate a molecular mechanism to be eligible if it reports a factor-specific association with a radiation outcome. An original article that includes planning or dosimetry will be retained if it also meets the biological eligibility rule. Publication type and topic exclusions will be applied during documented screening, rather than as broad NOT clauses in the search, to avoid inadvertently losing mixed-content original studies.

### Search design and frozen source acquisition

Two PubMed strategies will be run: a general ionizing-radiation and cancer-response search and a focused proton-beam search. Their exact executable expressions, field tags and date clause are specified in `RadBioNET_v2_PubMed_searches.txt`. The two streams will be united by PMID while retaining both stream labels. The strategies will be piloted against a prespecified set of at least 50 known eligible studies covering photon, proton, carbon-ion and mixed-particle experiments, preclinical and clinical contexts, toxicity and immune outcomes. Missing sentinel reports will be investigated before the query is frozen. Any resulting changes will be versioned, with the original and revised query strings and reasons preserved. Search sensitivity measured against this sentinel set will be reported as a development check rather than a population recall estimate.

At execution, the team will save the UTC date and time, PubMed database, full submitted query, PubMed Search Details translation, interface or E-utilities version, query count, and the complete returned PMID list for every stream and interval. Because PubMed ESearch exposes at most the first 10,000 IDs for one query, each stream will be partitioned by non-overlapping publication-date intervals. Intervals returning 10,000 or more records will be split recursively until every interval can be completely retrieved. The sum of interval counts will be compared with the number of retrieved IDs before deduplication. For each PMID, the PubMed XML record and original title, abstract, DOI, publication type, publication dates, journal and retraction or correction links will be saved. XML will be obtained in batches with EFetch. API requests will observe NCBI's published rate limit, include registered `tool` and `email` parameters and use an API key only if authorized. The source XML, query logs, PMID lists and exported intermediate tables will be immutable after the input freeze, with SHA-256 hashes in a manifest. Later PubMed updates will create a new input snapshot rather than silently changing the current one.

The retained historical NBIB exports and Zotero tables will be imported as a separate legacy stream. Their original byte sequence, path, hash and observed metadata will be preserved. Historical rows will be linked to the new PubMed snapshot using PMID first, then a verified DOI, then manual review of title, year and journal. A historical row will never be silently dropped because it is absent from a new search result. It will receive a documented eligibility or reconciliation status. The historical PubMed specification and its uncertain execution metadata will be cited as provenance for the legacy stream, while the newly executed queries will define the v2 retrieval. Sources beyond PubMed may be added only through a separately documented search and export protocol.

### Identity resolution and screening

PMID will be the primary publication identifier for records obtained from PubMed. DOI values will be preserved verbatim and also normalized for matching by trimming whitespace, removing a leading DOI resolver or `doi:` prefix, case-folding and removing only extraneous terminal punctuation after verifying the raw string. DOI will be treated as an opaque identifier, never split on semicolons. A DOI collision that maps to distinct PMIDs, or several DOIs for one PMID, will be queued for manual resolution. Rows without a verified PMID will be compared using normalized title, publication year, first author and journal; title similarity alone will not merge records. Every merge decision will retain the input IDs, rule, reviewer and timestamp. A stable publication ID will be assigned, and all original IDs and source-stream memberships will be retained in an alias table.

Two reviewers will independently screen each unique citation at title and abstract level against the frozen inclusion rules. Records with inadequate abstracts or uncertain eligibility will advance to full-text screening. For every excluded full text, one primary exclusion reason will be selected from a prespecified hierarchy: retracted; duplicate report of the same results; no cancer or radiotherapy context; non-ionizing exposure; no original data; no factor-specific response; or inaccessible content that prevents a decision. Reviews, methods papers and planning-only studies will be represented by the relevant reason. Disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer, with both initial decisions retained. Corrections, expressions of concern and retraction notices will be checked against PubMed metadata and linked to the affected article. Retracted articles will not contribute accepted relations; correction notices will be recorded and their effect on relevant claims reviewed. The screening log will report the number of retrieved records, stream overlap, duplicates, records screened, full texts assessed, each exclusion reason, included publications and included publications with no accepted relation.

### Frozen annotation codebook and curator training

Before production annotation, a codebook will define each field, allowed values, evidence location, decision boundary and examples of positive, negative and ambiguous cases. Curators will independently annotate a pilot set of 100 eligible publications deliberately covering multiple particles, combined treatments, cell and patient studies, toxicity and fractionated dose expressions. Disagreements will be used to refine the codebook, and the revised version will be frozen before production. Pilot publications will be reannotated under the frozen codebook if included in the release. Every later codebook change will have a version identifier, affected fields, justification and a list of records for re-review.

The codebook will separate (1) the **factor entity** from (2) its **role** in the reported comparison, (3) the **perturbation** applied to it, (4) the **measured outcome**, and (5) the **direction of the radiation-linked effect**. Roles will include manipulated target, administered drug or agent, biomarker or measured correlate, drug target and pathway context. Perturbations will include inhibition, knockdown or knockout, overexpression or activation, administration, depletion, observed level and not reported. A drug will not be replaced by its molecular target in the relation key. Observational associations will be labelled as associations and will not be converted into causal sensitization claims. The direction vocabulary and its comparator requirement will be fixed before production. Tumour radiosensitization, tumour radioresistance, normal-tissue toxicity increase and normal-tissue toxicity reduction will be distinct values. Immune activation, immunosuppression, DNA-damage change, expression change and null response will be recorded as outcome classes or directional modifiers when the underlying comparison supports them. A record may carry more than one outcome only when each outcome has a separately identified supporting span; the normalized relation table will store them as separate rows. A null label will describe the tested comparison and reported non-detection, without claiming proof of no biological effect.

### Text acquisition and candidate generation

Titles and abstracts will be taken from the frozen PubMed XML. Full text will be used when lawfully accessible and when an abstract does not resolve the relationship, experimental arm, factor role or dose role. The text source will be identified as title, abstract, article section, table or figure legend, with a stable section or table/figure locator. The source expression will be preserved in its original wording for internal curation. A matching copy will undergo Unicode normalization, whitespace normalization and sentence segmentation. The software, language model if any, version and dictionary release will be fixed in a lock file. Automated term matching will generate candidates and never by itself assign accepted evidence.

Factor aliases will be mapped to frozen reference vocabularies. Human genes will use an HGNC ID and approved symbol where a unique assignment is possible; chemicals will use a ChEBI ID or another named, versioned public identifier; cancer concepts will use a versioned NCI Thesaurus code when resolvable; cell lines will use a Cellosaurus accession when reported. The raw phrase, normalized label, namespace, identifier, dictionary version and mapping status will all be retained. Ambiguous abbreviations and symbols, species-dependent gene names, drug mixtures and pathways without a single external identifier will be reviewed rather than force-mapped. The factor dictionary will contain explicit exclusions for false matches and will retain the source and date of each alias. The original display label will be maintained for comparison with RadBioNET v1.

Candidate generation will combine dictionary terms and response expressions within a sentence, adjacent sentences or an explicit cross-reference to a table or figure. The matcher will record every matched span, the exact pattern and version, and sentence or section boundaries. Co-occurrence in the same sentence will be a candidate-support feature, not a quality verdict or a factual relation. Candidates pairing several factors with several direction terms in one sentence will remain separate review tasks. A cross-sentence relation will be accepted only when a curator verifies explicit coreference or a clearly connected result statement. Negated, hypothetical, background-only and purely methodological mentions will not be promoted to accepted relations. A record may be marked `uncertain` when the source cannot resolve the factor, comparison or irradiation context; such records will be distributed in a separate candidate or uncertainty file, not counted as supported relations.

### Manual relation adjudication and evidence capture

Two curators will independently evaluate every proposed positive relation using the source text, and a third curator will adjudicate differences. Reviewers will see the candidate factor, evidence spans and source article, but not each other's decision. For a relation to be accepted, reviewers must identify the relevant factor and role, intervention or observed state, irradiation arm or its unresolved status, explicit outcome and comparator, direction, and an evidence locator. For an experimental radiosensitization label, the comparison must show a greater tumour radiation response under the stated factor perturbation than under radiation without that perturbation, or an equally explicit author-reported comparison. For radioresistance the orientation is reversed. An observational biomarker association will retain its observational role even if associated with sensitivity. Increased normal-tissue injury and reduced normal-tissue injury will be coded separately from tumour response; immune activation alone will not be recoded as tumour radiosensitization. The source evidence must support the same comparison rather than separate claims from different arms or experiments. Where full text is unavailable, the relation may be accepted from the title or abstract only if these elements are explicit there. Otherwise it remains uncertain. For each decision, the system will preserve the candidate ID, both raw decisions, adjudicated decision, reason code, reviewer IDs, timestamps and the codebook version. Disagreements will not be overwritten.

The relation's evidence-location field will distinguish direct same-sentence support, a linked adjacent sentence, a linked table/figure and a publication-level mention. Only the first three can support an accepted arm-level relationship when the referents are explicit. Publication-level mentions can support bibliographic context but not assign a particular particle, dose or disease subtype to every relation in the paper. Supporting text will be stored or redistributed only where rights permit. When quotation is restricted, the public record will retain PMID or DOI, section/table/figure locator and a concise curator-authored evidence note, with exact spans held only in the restricted curation record.

### Radiation exposure, arm and dose model

Each irradiation arm will have a stable arm ID linked to its experiment or clinical comparison. The arm table will distinguish radiation particle category, specific particle or beam, delivery modality, equipment when reported, target or irradiated material, dose role, schedule and radiation units. Particle categories will include photon, proton, carbon ion, other heavy ion, neutron, electron, alpha and unresolved. Photon subtype will distinguish X-ray and gamma ray only when stated. Delivery techniques such as IMRT, SBRT, brachytherapy and FLASH will be stored in a modality field rather than treated as particle types. Physical absorbed dose in Gy, centigray values converted to Gy, biologically weighted dose reported as Gy(RBE), dose to an organ at risk, total prescribed dose and experimental exposure will be represented by separate unit and role fields. No default RBE value will be inferred. Missing particle or dose details will not be copied from the paper's title, a different study arm or a generic Methods paragraph into an unrelated relation.

Particle attribution will be coded from an explicit particle mention in the relation's result or arm description, a verified link to a labelled arm in Methods, a title-only or publication-level mention, or unresolved conflicting exposures. The first two may populate the relation's particle field after curator confirmation. Title-only and publication-level mentions will populate paper context and flag the relation for review; they will not by themselves disambiguate a multi-arm experiment. If photon and proton arms share the same factor, they will yield separate relation records only when the response can be linked to each arm. If no arm-level link can be made, the relation's particle field will remain unresolved. An evidence-level code will record how particle identity was assigned independently of the relation's scientific validity.

The dose parser will first detect explicit fractionation expressions and their spans. It will recognize patterns such as `3 × 8 Gy`, `8 Gy × 3`, `3 fractions of 8 Gy`, `8 Gy in 3 fractions` and `60 Gy in 30 fractions`. It will store dose per fraction, number of fractions and total dose as separate values when the text specifies enough information, and it will preserve the original text. Next it will detect single doses and ranges, including `250 cGy`, `0–8 Gy` and `60 Gy(RBE)`. A range will remain lower and upper bounds; a midpoint will not be substituted in the primary table. A `0 Gy` control will be retained as an unirradiated comparator rather than treated as treatment dose. Values in Gy(RBE) will remain distinct from physical Gy, and biological weighting will not be removed. Implausible or syntactically ambiguous parses will be reviewed against the source. Dose mentions will receive a role such as tumour or experimental exposure, organ-at-risk dose, prescribed total, per-fraction dose, control, or unclear. The parser will link a dose to an irradiation arm only when the same source text or a verified Methods-to-results reference establishes that link. An article-level dose mention will remain unassigned when several arms could be intended. The exact parser version, test cases and unresolved cases will be released with the code.

### Publication and relation context

Cancer site and subtype, species, model, cell line, experimental method, toxicity endpoint and clinical outcome will be recorded at the narrowest supported level. A title-level cancer term will not automatically populate every factor–arm relation from a paper that studies several diseases or models. Cancer and model labels will be linked to an experiment or relation only after a curator confirms the supporting source span. An organ toxicity label will identify the affected normal tissue and measured endpoint. The label will not imply whether a factor increased or reduced toxicity; that direction requires a separate comparison. Evidence type will describe study design or material, for example cell experiment, animal experiment, organoid, patient cohort or trial. It will not be used as a surrogate for risk of bias, clinical effectiveness or relation confidence. Assay labels such as clonogenic survival, RNA sequencing and immunohistochemistry will be linked to the outcome or experiment where possible. A missing field will be encoded as `not_reported`, `not_applicable` or `ambiguous`, never silently replaced with an invented value.

### Reconciliation of the existing RadBioNET workbook

Each v1 row will receive a stable legacy row ID based on its original release file and row number. The original workbook, RDS and derived outputs will remain immutable. Legacy fields will be mapped to the new schema through a versioned crosswalk. The historical `Quality flag` and `Particle-specific verdict` will be retained as legacy provenance, not treated as accuracy grades. The 15,720 legacy rows will be classified as one-to-one mapped, split into several arm- or outcome-specific records, merged because of exact duplication, retained as uncertain, excluded with reason, or awaiting review. Many-to-many mapping will be allowed when one historical row contains several directions or particle categories. The mapping table will retain the original row ID, new record IDs, reason, reviewer and date. Existing name corrections and particle edits will be re-evaluated against source evidence; no legacy value will be accepted solely because it appears in the workbook. Version counts and changes will be derived from the mapping table.

### Quality control and technical validation

All processing stages will generate machine-readable counts, unique-key checks and exception logs. Automated checks will confirm that every accepted relation resolves to one publication, factor entity, experiment or comparison, outcome, evidence locator and review decision; that each arm-specific dose and particle value has an arm link; that direction and outcome vocabulary values are allowed; and that no retracted report contributes a positive relation. Numeric dose checks will enforce nonnegative values, valid units and internally consistent total = dose per fraction × fraction count where all three are reported, allowing for source rounding. A fixed suite of positive and negative parser examples will cover `3 × 8 Gy`, `60 Gy in 30 fractions`, `60 Gy(RBE)`, cGy conversion, ranges, organ-at-risk dose and multi-arm papers. A full clean-room rebuild from frozen inputs and a locked software environment will reproduce the release files and checksum manifest. Any manual correction will be entered through a versioned decision log, not by untracked spreadsheet editing.

Pre-adjudication reviewer agreement will be reported separately for screening, relation acceptance, factor role, direction, particle assignment, outcome class and dose role. The denominator for each field will be reported. Categorical agreement will include observed agreement and an appropriate chance-adjusted measure; span annotations will use exact or overlap-based agreement with the criterion specified. Adjudicated results will be reported separately from initial agreement. A blinded post-adjudication audit will draw a probability sample of 400 accepted relation records, stratified by input stream and broad particle category, with known inclusion probabilities. An additional 100 deliberately high-risk records may be inspected for error discovery but will not be pooled unweighted with the probability sample. An independent reviewer will check factor identity and role, relation support, direction, arm-linked particle, dose value and role, and context against the cited source. Field-specific accuracy and 95% confidence intervals will be reported using the sample design and clustering by publication where necessary. A sample of screened-out citations and a sample of included papers will be independently examined for missed eligible reports or relations; recall will only be estimated if this sample defines a valid denominator. Identified errors will be corrected, their category and impact logged, and affected records re-reviewed. The final validation report will provide both counts and examples of unresolved cases. Sample size and any internal release threshold are project decisions, not requirements imposed by *Scientific Data*.

### Data serialization, versioning and availability

The release will include `publications`, `search_runs`, `screening_decisions`, `experiments`, `irradiation_arms`, `dose_mentions`, `factors`, `evidence_relations`, `annotation_decisions`, `legacy_crosswalk`, `excluded_or_uncertain_candidates`, `validation_sample`, a data dictionary, codebook, query files, software lock file, checksums and change log. Foreign keys and controlled vocabularies will be documented. Every accepted relation will have a stable, nonsemantic identifier assigned once and preserved through corrections. Source identifiers, raw expressions, normalized labels and annotation version will remain accessible. A flat compatibility export may be generated for the existing website, but its document-level fields will not be represented as arm-level facts. The exact frozen archive, data license, code version and citation will be deposited in a repository that supplies a persistent identifier and anonymous access for peer review. Public redistribution of article excerpts will be checked against source rights. A new update will receive a new release identifier and a machine-readable change log, leaving prior releases available.
